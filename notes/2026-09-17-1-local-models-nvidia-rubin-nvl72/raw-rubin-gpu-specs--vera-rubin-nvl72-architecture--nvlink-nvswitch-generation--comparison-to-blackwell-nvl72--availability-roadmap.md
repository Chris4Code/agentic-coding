# NVIDIA Rubin / Vera Rubin NVL72 — Research Export (2026-09-17)

Deep-research pass extending the "Local Models" chapter's "Datacenter GPU Stacks" section beyond the existing A100/H100/H200/B200/GB200 note (2026-08-29). Focus: NVIDIA's Rubin generation and the Vera Rubin NVL72 rack-scale system, as they stand as of 2026-09-17.

## 1. Rubin GPU specs

**Naming and product family.** NVIDIA's Rubin generation ships as (at least) three distinct silicon SKUs, which is easy to conflate: the standard **Rubin GPU** (compute GPU for the Vera Rubin NVL72 rack), **Rubin CPX** (a separate, GDDR7-based chip purpose-built for long-context "prefill"/attention-heavy inference, announced as its own product), and **Rubin Ultra** (the H2-2027 follow-on SKU, a quad-die package). These are covered in turn below. [NVIDIA investor press release — "Kicks Off the Next Generation of AI With Rubin"](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Kicks-Off-the-Next-Generation-of-AI-With-Rubin--Six-New-Chips-One-Incredible-AI-Supercomputer/default.aspx) (primary) names Rubin GPU, Vera CPU, NVLink 6 Switch, ConnectX-9, BlueField-4, and Spectrum-6 as "six new chips"; Rubin CPX was announced separately. [NVIDIA Newsroom — Rubin CPX unveil](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-unveils-rubin-cpx-a-new-class-of-gpu-designed-for-massive-context-inference) (primary).

**Process node, packaging, die layout.** The Rubin GPU is a multi-chip-module built on a TSMC 3nm-class node (reported as "N3P" by secondary outlets), using CoWoS-L packaging. Secondary reporting agrees the design sits at the reticle limit but differs slightly on exact die accounting: some describe "two reticle-sized compute dies," while a more detailed breakdown describes **two near-reticle-sized compute dies plus two I/O dies** on a 4x-reticle CoWoS-L interposer, alongside eight HBM4 stacks. This is corroborated-secondary but with inconsistent granularity across outlets — treat the "2 compute + 2 I/O die" figure as more detailed but not primary-confirmed. [TechPowerUp — "NVIDIA Details the Rubin Architecture"](https://www.techpowerup.com/350947/nvidia-details-the-rubin-architecture-die-annotation-vera-cpu-hbm4-and-disaggregated-inference), [wccftech — Rubin GPU architecture, 336B transistors](https://wccftech.com/nvidia-rubin-gpu-architecture/), [Barrack AI — Rubin at GTC 2026 technical breakdown](https://blog.barrack.ai/nvidia-rubin-specs-architecture-2026/), [Thunder Compute — Rubin architecture](https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/nvidia-rubin-architecture).

**Transistor count.** 336 billion transistors, reported consistently as "1.6x Blackwell's 208 billion" across multiple independent secondary outlets — corroborated-secondary (wccftech, tech-insider.org, TechPowerUp, Barrack AI, Thunder Compute all cite the same figure). This number does not appear in the primary NVIDIA press materials fetched for this research, so despite wide agreement it remains corroborated-secondary rather than primary-confirmed. [tech-insider.org — "NVIDIA Rubin GPU: 336B Transistors"](https://tech-insider.org/nvidia-gtc-2026-rubin-gpu-analysis/), [wccftech](https://wccftech.com/nvidia-rubin-gpu-architecture/).

**Compute units.** Secondary sources describe 224 Streaming Multiprocessors with sixth-generation Tensor Cores supporting FP4, FP6, FP8, FP16, BF16, TF32, FP32, and FP64 — corroborated-secondary (TechPowerUp, wccftech), not confirmed on NVIDIA's own pages in this research pass.

**Memory (HBM4).** Secondary sources state **up to 288 GB HBM4 per GPU across 8 stacks, ~22 TB/s bandwidth**. [Barrack AI](https://blog.barrack.ai/nvidia-rubin-specs-architecture-2026/), [Thunder Compute](https://www.thundercompute.com/blog/nvidia-rubin-architecture), [TechPowerUp](https://www.techpowerup.com/350947/nvidia-details-the-rubin-architecture-die-annotation-vera-cpu-hbm4-and-disaggregated-inference). Cross-checking against NVIDIA's own primary rack-level figures for the Vera Rubin NVL72 (20.7 TB HBM4 total, 72 GPUs — see §2) gives 20.7 TB / 72 ≈ **287.5 GB/GPU**, which matches the 288 GB figure almost exactly (primary-corroborated for capacity). However, dividing NVIDIA's primary rack bandwidth figure (1,400 TB/s total ÷ 72 GPUs ≈ **19.4 TB/s/GPU**) does **not** match the secondary "22 TB/s" figure. **Research Note: this bandwidth-per-GPU figure is not well corroborated — the commonly repeated "22 TB/s" number from secondary outlets conflicts with the per-GPU bandwidth implied by NVIDIA's own published rack-level total.**

**Compute (FLOPS).** NVIDIA's own investor press release states the Rubin GPU delivers **"50 petaflops of NVFP4 compute for AI inference"** — primary-sourced. A NVFP4 training figure of "35 PFLOPS," framed by one outlet as "5x and 3.5x higher than Blackwell" respectively, appears only in secondary reporting (Barrack AI) and was not found in NVIDIA's own materials in this pass — treat as single-source/unconfirmed. FP32/FP32-Tensor-Core figures of "130 TFLOPS" / "200 TFLOPS" reported by one spec-sheet aggregator (flopper.io) are single-source and not corroborated elsewhere — flag as unverified.

**Transformer Engine.** NVIDIA's own materials describe a "third-generation Transformer Engine with hardware-accelerated adaptive compression" on the Rubin GPU — primary. [Investor press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Kicks-Off-the-Next-Generation-of-AI-With-Rubin--Six-New-Chips-One-Incredible-AI-Supercomputer/default.aspx).

**Rubin CPX (distinct SKU).** Purpose-built for massive-context inference (attention/prefill-heavy workloads), using **128 GB GDDR7** (not HBM) at roughly 2 TB/s bandwidth, up to **30 PFLOPS NVFP4**, with dedicated attention-processing hardware claimed to be "3x faster" than Blackwell-generation attention compute, plus integrated video encode/decode. All primary-sourced. [NVIDIA Newsroom — Rubin CPX](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-unveils-rubin-cpx-a-new-class-of-gpu-designed-for-massive-context-inference). Notably, NVIDIA's launch quotes for Rubin CPX name Cursor and Magic (AI coding-assistant companies) as design partners explicitly for code-generation and large-codebase-context use cases — directly relevant to agentic coding: Cursor CEO Michael Truell and Magic CEO Eric Steinberger are both quoted in the primary release.

**Rubin Ultra (separate SKU, launching later).** Distinguished from the base Rubin GPU by being a **quad-die package** (four GPU dies vs. two for base Rubin), reported at up to **100 PFLOPS FP4** per package and **1 TB of HBM4e** per package — corroborated-secondary across several outlets. [Tom's Hardware — "Nvidia announces Rubin GPUs in 2026, Rubin Ultra in 2027, Feynman also added to roadmap"](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/nvidia-announces-rubin-gpus-in-2026-rubin-ultra-in-2027-feynam-after), [DataCenterDynamics — Rubin Ultra NVL576 600kW](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nvidias-rubin-ultra-nvl576-rack-expected-to-be-600kw-coming-second-half-of-2027/), [tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/nvidia-gtc-2026-rubin-gpu-analysis/). Full rack-level Rubin Ultra figures are covered in §5.

**Launch timing.** NVIDIA's own press release states Rubin-based partner products/systems will be "available from partners the second half of 2026" — primary. Secondary press describes Rubin as having entered "full production" ahead of schedule, though outlets disagree on exactly when/where this milestone was announced (see §5 for the date conflict). [Introl — "NVIDIA Rubin Enters Full Production"](https://introl.com/blog/nvidia-rubin-full-production-ces-2026-ai-infrastructure).

## 2. Vera Rubin NVL72 architecture

**Rack composition.** NVIDIA's own product pages confirm the rack is **72 Rubin GPUs + 36 Vera CPUs**, i.e. 36 "Vera Rubin Superchip" modules of 1 Vera CPU + 2 Rubin GPUs each — directly analogous to the GB200 NVL72's Grace+2×Blackwell superchip pattern already documented in the 2026-08-29 note. Primary-sourced. [NVIDIA DGX Vera Rubin NVL72 datasheet](https://resources.nvidia.com/en-us-dgx-systems/dgx-vera-rubin-nvl72-datasheet), [NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 product page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-rubin-nvl72). **This confirms the "72 Rubin GPUs" framing refers to 72 GPU packages (36 superchips × 2 GPUs each), the same accounting convention NVIDIA used for GB200 NVL72** — not 72 individual compute dies (each Rubin GPU package itself contains 2 compute dies, per §1, so the rack totals roughly 144 compute dies, though NVIDIA's own marketing counts "GPUs" at the package level).

**Vera CPU.** 88 custom "Olympus" Arm cores per CPU, Armv9.2-compatible — primary. [Investor press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Kicks-Off-the-Next-Generation-of-AI-With-Rubin--Six-New-Chips-One-Incredible-AI-Supercomputer/default.aspx). This is cross-validated by the rack-level figure on NVIDIA's own product page of **"3,168 custom NVIDIA Olympus cores | 6,336 Threads"** for the full rack: 3,168 ÷ 36 CPUs = exactly 88 cores/CPU, confirming internal consistency between two NVIDIA primary sources. [NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 product page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-rubin-nvl72). CPU memory: up to 54 TB LPDDR5X across the rack (primary, same page).

**Rack memory (GPU side).** **20.7 TB HBM4 total, 1,400 TB/s aggregate bandwidth** — stated identically on both NVIDIA's DGX datasheet and the Vera Rubin NVL72 product page — primary, doubly confirmed. [DGX Vera Rubin NVL72 datasheet](https://resources.nvidia.com/en-us-dgx-systems/dgx-vera-rubin-nvl72-datasheet), [NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 product page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-rubin-nvl72).

**Rack compute (FLOPS), all primary, explicitly marked "preliminary" by NVIDIA itself:**
- NVFP4 inference: 3,600 PFLOPS
- NVFP4 training: 2,520 PFLOPS
- FP8/FP6 training: 1,260 PFLOPS
- BF16/FP16: 288 PFLOPS
- FP32: 9,360 TFLOPS
- FP64: 2,400 TFLOPS

[NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 product page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-rubin-nvl72). **Research Note: NVIDIA labels all of these figures "preliminary information, subject to change" on its own spec sheet — they are primary-sourced but explicitly not finalized.**

**NVLink-C2C (CPU↔GPU) bandwidth.** NVIDIA's product page states rack-aggregate NVLink-C2C bandwidth of **65 TB/s**; dividing by 36 CPUs gives 1.8 TB/s per CPU-to-GPU link, which matches a secondary claim that this "doubles the C2C bandwidth over previous generations" (Grace-Blackwell was ~900 GB/s). Primary aggregate figure, secondary per-unit interpretation — consistent. [NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 product page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-rubin-nvl72); secondary corroboration via [Medium/Online Inference — "NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72: Architecture, specs, and AI factory scaling"](https://medium.com/online-inference/nvidia-vera-rubin-nvl72-architecture-specs-and-ai-factory-scaling-030e6eceddb5).

**Power and cooling.** NVIDIA's own pages disclose only a **45°C inlet temperature** spec (confirming full liquid cooling) but do **not** publish an official rack-level kW figure in the materials fetched for this research. Secondary estimates vary and are not settled: figures reported across different outlets range from roughly **180 kW to 230 kW** per rack, with one describing "DLC-2 direct liquid cooling... sized for 227 kW per rack." **Research Note: the exact power draw of a Vera Rubin NVL72 rack is not well corroborated — secondary sources disagree by roughly 50 kW, and NVIDIA has not published an official figure in the sources checked.** [Hashrate Index — Vera Rubin NVL72 full specs](https://hashrateindex.com/blog/nvidia-vera-rubin-nvl72-specs-breakdown/).

**"100 MW AI Factory" scale figure.** NVIDIA's product page also cites an aggregate, multi-rack "100 MW AI Factory" configuration delivering "up to 42 PB" of fast memory — this is a data-center-scale marketing figure, not a single-rack spec, and should not be confused with the 20.7 TB single-rack figure above. Primary. [NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 product page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-rubin-nvl72).

## 3. NVLink / NVSwitch generation

**Generation.** NVLink 6 ships with Rubin/Vera Rubin — confirmed primary, named as one of the "six new chips" in NVIDIA's own press release. [Investor press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Kicks-Off-the-Next-Generation-of-AI-With-Rubin--Six-New-Chips-One-Incredible-AI-Supercomputer/default.aspx), [NVIDIA developer blog — "How NVIDIA NVLink 6 Delivers Multi-Layer Resiliency"](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-nvidia-nvlink-6-delivers-multi-layer-resiliency-for-ai-factories/).

**Per-GPU bandwidth.** **3.6 TB/s bidirectional per GPU** — stated identically in both the investor press release and the NVIDIA developer blog (primary, doubly confirmed). This is 2x the Blackwell-generation NVLink 5 figure of 1.8 TB/s/GPU already documented in the chapter's existing NVLink note, consistent with the generational doubling pattern.

**Aggregate rack bandwidth — conflicting primary figures.** The investor press release and developer blog both state the Vera Rubin NVL72 rack achieves **"260 TB/s"** of aggregate GPU-to-GPU bandwidth. However, NVIDIA's own Vera Rubin NVL72 *product/spec* page (fetched directly for this research) lists **"NVLink Bandwidth: 216 TB/s"** for the same rack. **Research Note: this is a direct conflict between two of NVIDIA's own primary sources (260 TB/s in press/developer-blog materials vs. 216 TB/s on the product spec page) — this note cannot resolve which figure is current/authoritative, and both are reported here rather than silently picking one.** [Investor press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Kicks-Off-the-Next-Generation-of-AI-With-Rubin--Six-New-Chips-One-Incredible-AI-Supercomputer/default.aspx), [NVIDIA developer blog](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/how-nvidia-nvlink-6-delivers-multi-layer-resiliency-for-ai-factories/), [NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 product page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-rubin-nvl72). A secondary outlet's headline also reports "260 TB/s Scale-Up Bandwidth," aligning with the higher figure. [VideoCardz — "NVIDIA Vera Rubin NVL72 Detailed: 72 GPUs, 36 CPUs, 260 TB/s Scale-Up Bandwidth"](https://videocardz.com/newz/nvidia-vera-rubin-nvl72-detailed-72-gpus-36-cpus-260-tb-s-scale-up-bandwidth).

**NVSwitch.** NVIDIA's own datasheet confirms **9× "L1" NVLink Switches** per rack — primary. [DGX Vera Rubin NVL72 datasheet](https://resources.nvidia.com/en-us-dgx-systems/dgx-vera-rubin-nvl72-datasheet). Secondary reporting adds detail (each switch tray containing four NVLink 6 switch chips, ~28.8 TB/s per tray, and in-network compute of "14.4 TFLOPS FP8") that could not be traced to a primary NVIDIA source in this pass — **flag as single-source/unverified**, and note that per-link accounting given by some secondary summaries (e.g. "32 links per GPU at 200 GB/s") does not cleanly arithmetically reconcile to the stated 3.6 TB/s per-GPU figure (32 × 200 GB/s = 6.4 TB/s raw, not 3.6 TB/s), suggesting this particular secondary breakdown is imprecise or describes a different counting convention (e.g., unidirectional vs. bidirectional, or fewer active links).

**Scale-up domain size.** The standard Vera Rubin NVL72 keeps the same 72-GPU single-NVLink-domain size as Blackwell's GB200/GB300 NVL72 — no increase at this tier. The domain size only grows with **Rubin Ultra's "Kyber" NVL576 rack** (576 GPU dies / 144 quad-die packages in one scale-up domain), an 8x increase over NVL72 — corroborated-secondary (see §5). [DataCenterDynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nvidias-rubin-ultra-nvl576-rack-expected-to-be-600kw-coming-second-half-of-2027/).

**Scale-out networking.** ConnectX-9 SuperNICs at 800 Gb/s per port (>144 single-port OSFP ConnectX-9 per rack) and BlueField-4 DPUs at 400 Gb/s (>18 dual-port per rack), plus Spectrum-6 Ethernet switches with co-packaged optics for scale-out beyond the rack — primary. [DGX Vera Rubin NVL72 datasheet](https://resources.nvidia.com/en-us-dgx-systems/dgx-vera-rubin-nvl72-datasheet), [Investor press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Kicks-Off-the-Next-Generation-of-AI-With-Rubin--Six-New-Chips-One-Incredible-AI-Supercomputer/default.aspx).

## 4. Comparison to Blackwell NVL72 (GB200 / GB300)

**GB300 NVL72 exists as an intermediate "Blackwell Ultra" generation**, sitting between GB200 NVL72 and Vera Rubin NVL72 in NVIDIA's annual cadence (see §5). It uses B300 "Blackwell Ultra" GPUs with 288 GB HBM3e each (20 TB/rack) vs. GB200's ~186 GB/GPU (~13.4 TB/rack) — a roughly 1.5x memory-capacity increase at the GPU level. Corroborated-secondary across multiple independent outlets. [Introl — "Why NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 Blackwell Ultra Matters"](https://introl.com/blog/why-nvidia-gb300-nvl72-blackwell-ultra-matters), [SemiAnalysis InferenceX — GB300 NVL72 chip page](https://inferencex.semianalysis.com/chips/gb300-nvl72), [Verda — GB300 NVL72 architecture](https://verda.com/blog/gb300-nvl72-architecture), [ModulEdge — GB300 NVL72 specs](https://www.moduledge.com/blog/gb300-nvl72).
- Compute: GB300 delivers 1,080 PFLOPS dense NVFP4 vs. GB200's 720 PFLOPS dense (1.5x); both reach 1,440 PFLOPS on sparse FP4 (identical) — corroborated-secondary.
- Power: GB300 NVL72 draws roughly 135–142 kW vs. GB200's ~120 kW — corroborated-secondary.
- Networking: GB300 doubles scale-out bandwidth via ConnectX-8 (800 Gb/s) vs. GB200's ConnectX-7 (400 Gb/s) — corroborated-secondary.
- Availability: GB300 NVL72 from partners in H2 2025, vs. GB200 NVL72 already generally available — corroborated-secondary.

**NVIDIA's own claimed generational deltas, Vera Rubin NVL72 vs. GB200/GB300 NVL72 (all primary-sourced quotes):**
- "Up to 10x reduction in inference token cost" vs. Blackwell [investor press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Kicks-Off-the-Next-Generation-of-AI-With-Rubin--Six-New-Chips-One-Incredible-AI-Supercomputer/default.aspx) / restated as "one-tenth the cost per million tokens" on the [Vera Rubin NVL72 product page](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/vera-rubin-nvl72) — same claim, two primary sources, consistent.
- "4x reduction in the number of GPUs to train MoE models" (investor release) / "one-fourth the number of GPUs" (product page) — same claim in both, consistent.
- "18x faster assembly and servicing than Blackwell" — primary (investor release); this is an operational/mechanical claim, not a compute claim.
- "Up to 10x more tokens per megawatt" (product page) and Spectrum-X networking "5x better power efficiency" (investor release) — primary, both framed as efficiency (perf/watt) claims.

**Memory/bandwidth deltas, quantified.** Vera Rubin NVL72's 20.7 TB HBM4 rack total is only modestly larger than GB300 NVL72's 20 TB HBM3e rack total (roughly a 3.5% capacity increase) — **the generational leap here is much smaller on raw capacity than on bandwidth/compute/interconnect.** This is a notable, non-obvious finding: marketing headlines emphasize 10x inference-cost and token-per-megawatt improvements, but those gains come overwhelmingly from compute-density, FP4 precision, and interconnect/software improvements rather than from a large jump in per-rack memory capacity versus the immediately-preceding GB300 Blackwell Ultra rack.

**Rubin CPX-equipped rack vs. GB300 NVL72.** NVIDIA's own Rubin CPX materials claim the (separate, disaggregated-inference) **Vera Rubin NVL144 CPX** platform delivers **"7.5x more AI performance than NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 systems"**, with 8 exaflops of AI compute, 100 TB of fast memory, and 1.7 PB/s of memory bandwidth in one rack — primary-sourced. **Research Note: this comparison is not apples-to-apples with the standard Vera Rubin NVL72 vs. GB300 NVL72 comparison above** — the NVL144 CPX platform is a different, disaggregated rack design mixing Rubin CPX (GDDR7) and standard Rubin GPUs with Vera CPUs, purpose-built for long-context inference, so its 7.5x claim should not be read as representative of the standard Vera Rubin NVL72 vs. GB300 delta. [NVIDIA Newsroom — Rubin CPX](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-unveils-rubin-cpx-a-new-class-of-gpu-designed-for-massive-context-inference).

## 5. Availability / roadmap

**Annual cadence, confirmed at GTC 2025 (March 2025).** Jensen Huang formalized NVIDIA's move from a roughly biennial to an **annual architecture cadence**: Blackwell (2024) → Blackwell Ultra/B300 (H2 2025) → **Rubin/R100 (H2 2026)** → **Rubin Ultra/R300 (H2 2027)** → Feynman (2028). This cadence and naming sequence is corroborated-secondary across many independent outlets that all describe the same slide/announcement. [Digitimes — GTC 2025 roadmap](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20250319PD238/nvidia-gtc-rubin-2025-roadmap.html), [Tom's Hardware — Rubin/Rubin Ultra/Feynman roadmap](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/nvidia-announces-rubin-gpus-in-2026-rubin-ultra-in-2027-feynam-after), [Labo LLM — "Nvidia Roadmap 2025-2028"](https://www.labo-llm.fr/en/infrastructure/nvidia-roadmap-vera-rubin/), [VRLA Tech — NVIDIA GPU Roadmap 2026-2030](https://vrlatech.com/nvidia-gpu-roadmap-2026-2030/), [The Next Platform — "Driving Down The AI System Roadmap With Nvidia"](https://www.nextplatform.com/compute/2026/03/19/driving-down-the-ai-system-roadmap-with-nvidia/5210195).

**"Full production" milestone — conflicting dates/venues reported.** Secondary press disagrees on exactly when/where NVIDIA declared Rubin in "full production": one set of outlets ties this to **CES 2026 (January 2026)**, describing it as "well ahead of schedule," while another describes **GTC Taipei, June 1, 2026**. **Research Note: this specific milestone date is not well corroborated — different secondary outlets name different events, and this note could not confirm which (if either) is the NVIDIA-designated official "full production" announcement.** [Introl — "NVIDIA Rubin Enters Full Production"](https://introl.com/blog/nvidia-rubin-full-production-ces-2026-ai-infrastructure) (CES 2026), [wccftech — "Rubin AI Chips Enter Full Production Well Ahead of Schedule"](https://wccftech.com/nvidia-rubin-ai-chips-enter-full-production-well-ahead-of-schedule/) (CES 2026), [Next Wave Insight — "Nvidia Rubin in Production"](https://nextwavesinsight.com/nvidia-rubin-gpu-production-silicon-hyperscaler-2026/) (GTC Taipei, June 2026).

**General availability.** NVIDIA's own press release states partner systems/products will be "available... the second half of 2026" — primary, and consistent regardless of the full-production date conflict above. [Investor press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Kicks-Off-the-Next-Generation-of-AI-With-Rubin--Six-New-Chips-One-Incredible-AI-Supercomputer/default.aspx).

**Named early-access partners/customers — primary list.** NVIDIA's own press release names as partners/customers: Amazon Web Services, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, CoreWeave, Dell Technologies, HPE, Lenovo, Supermicro, xAI, Cohere, Mistral AI, and Perplexity. Primary. [Investor press release](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Kicks-Off-the-Next-Generation-of-AI-With-Rubin--Six-New-Chips-One-Incredible-AI-Supercomputer/default.aspx).

**Additional cloud names — secondary only.** A secondary synthesis additionally lists Lambda, Nebius, and Nscale as among "eight H2 2026 recipients" announced at CES 2026, alongside AWS/Google/Azure/OCI/CoreWeave. **These three (Lambda, Nebius, Nscale) were not found in the primary press release text fetched for this research — flag as secondary-only** until confirmed against a primary NVIDIA source. [Next Wave Insight](https://nextwavesinsight.com/nvidia-rubin-gpu-production-silicon-hyperscaler-2026/).

**2026 production volume / HBM4 yield — single-source, unconfirmed estimate.** One secondary outlet estimates NVIDIA's 2026 production ceiling at "200,000–300,000 Rubin GPUs" and states SK Hynix/Samsung began HBM4 mass production in Q4 2025 with yields "below mature HBM3e levels." **This is single-source and not an NVIDIA-disclosed figure — treat as speculative industry estimate, not fact.** [Next Wave Insight](https://nextwavesinsight.com/nvidia-rubin-gpu-production-silicon-hyperscaler-2026/).

**Rubin Ultra (R300) roadmap detail, H2 2027.** Corroborated-secondary across several independent outlets: quad-die Rubin Ultra packages, ~600 kW **"Kyber" NVL576** rack (144 quad-die packages = 576 GPU compute dies, 800V DC power distribution, vertical-tray modular design), 15 exaflops NVFP4 inference / 5 exaflops FP8 training per rack, 365 TB total memory, 4.6 PB/s HBM4e bandwidth, and NVLink 7 at "6x faster than Rubin" (~1.5 PB/s). Note that **rack naming for Rubin Ultra was inconsistent in earlier reporting** — one outlet's roadmap piece referred to a "Rubin Ultra NVL144" configuration, while the more detailed and more numerous later sources converge on **NVL576 ("Kyber")** as the actual Rubin Ultra rack design; the NVL144 label appears to be either an early/incorrect guess or a conflation with the unrelated Rubin-CPX-based "Vera Rubin NVL144 CPX" product (§1/§4), which is a real but different, earlier (2026) rack. [DataCenterDynamics — "Rubin Ultra NVL576... 600kW... H2 2027"](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nvidias-rubin-ultra-nvl576-rack-expected-to-be-600kw-coming-second-half-of-2027/), [Introl — "Vera Rubin: 600kW Racks by 2027"](https://introl.com/blog/nvidia-vera-rubin-gpu-600kw-racks-2027), [tech-insider.org](https://tech-insider.org/nvidia-gtc-2026-rubin-gpu-analysis/), [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/pc-components/gpus/nvidia-announces-rubin-gpus-in-2026-rubin-ultra-in-2027-feynam-after).

**Feynman (2028) — thin, early-stage information.** Announced at GTC 2025 as the generation after Rubin Ultra, targeted for TSMC's A16 (1.6nm-class) process and described as an "inference-first" design for long-context, multi-step agentic reasoning workloads. Corroborated-secondary on the existence/timing/process-node claims, but **this is 2+ years out from today (2026-09-17) and should be treated as an early, low-detail roadmap item** — no die/memory/compute specs have been published.
