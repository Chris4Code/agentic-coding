## Per-subtopic findings

**1. Rubin GPU specs — 🟡 mixed.** Product family has three distinct SKUs: base **Rubin GPU** (multi-chip module, TSMC N3P-class, CoWoS-L), **Rubin CPX** (separate GDDR7 chip for long-context inference), **Rubin Ultra** (quad-die, H2 2027). 🟢 Primary: NVFP4 inference compute "50 PFLOPS" per Rubin GPU; third-gen Transformer Engine; H2 2026 partner availability; Rubin CPX specs (128 GB GDDR7, ~2 TB/s, 30 PFLOPS NVFP4) including explicit Cursor/Magic design-partner quotes tying it to coding-agent use cases. 🟡 Corroborated-secondary: 336B transistors (1.6x Blackwell's 208B), 224 SMs, sixth-gen Tensor Cores. 🟠 Single-source/conflicting: "22 TB/s" per-GPU HBM4 bandwidth conflicts with dividing NVIDIA's own rack total (1,400 TB/s ÷ 72 ≈ 19.4 TB/s/GPU) — flagged, not resolved. NVFP4 training "35 PFLOPS" and FP32 figures are single-source.

**2. Vera Rubin NVL72 architecture — 🟢 strongly corroborated.** Confirms the "72 Rubin GPUs" framing = 72 GPU *packages* (36 superchips × 2 GPUs each, same package-level counting NVIDIA used for GB200), not 72 compute dies — each package holds 2 compute dies, so ~144 dies total. Vera CPU: 88 "Olympus" Arm cores each (cross-validated: 3,168 rack-total cores ÷ 36 = 88 exactly). Rack GPU memory: 20.7 TB HBM4 / 1,400 TB/s aggregate (doubly confirmed, two NVIDIA pages agree). Rack FLOPS (NVFP4 inference 3,600 PFLOPS, NVFP4 training 2,520 PFLOPS, etc.) are primary but explicitly marked "preliminary" by NVIDIA itself. 🟠 Power draw not officially published — secondary estimates range 180–230 kW/rack, unresolved.

**3. NVLink/NVSwitch generation — 🟢/🟠 mixed.** NVLink 6 confirmed primary, 3.6 TB/s bidirectional per GPU (2x NVLink 5's 1.8 TB/s) — doubly confirmed. 🟠 **Direct primary-vs-primary conflict**: rack aggregate bandwidth stated as both 260 TB/s (investor release, developer blog) and 216 TB/s (NVIDIA's own product spec page) — unresolved, both reported. 9× NVLink switches per rack is primary; per-tray switch-chip counts and in-network-compute TFLOPS are single-source and don't arithmetically reconcile with the per-GPU bandwidth figure. Scale-up domain stays at 72 GPUs for the standard rack — only Rubin Ultra's NVL576 "Kyber" rack (H2 2027) grows the domain, 8x larger.

**4. Comparison to Blackwell NVL72 — 🟢/🟡 mixed, one important caveat.** GB300 NVL72 ("Blackwell Ultra") is a real, corroborated-secondary intermediate generation between GB200 and Vera Rubin (288 GB HBM3e/GPU, 20 TB/rack, 1,080 PFLOPS dense NVFP4, ~135–142 kW). NVIDIA's own claimed deltas vs. Blackwell (all primary, consistent across two pages): "10x reduction in inference token cost," "4x fewer GPUs to train MoE," "10x more tokens per megawatt," "18x faster assembly." **Notable finding: raw HBM capacity barely grows** — 20.7 TB (Vera Rubin NVL72) vs. 20 TB (GB300 NVL72) is only ~3.5% more; the marketed 10x gains come from compute density/FP4/interconnect, not memory pool size. **Caveat flagged in raw file**: NVIDIA's "7.5x more AI performance than GB300 NVL72" claim is for a *different* product (Vera Rubin NVL144 CPX, a disaggregated rack mixing Rubin CPX + standard Rubin GPUs) — do not present this as the standard Vera Rubin NVL72 vs. GB300 delta.

**5. Availability/roadmap — 🟢 strongly corroborated (cadence), 🟠 thin (exact dates).** Annual cadence confirmed at GTC 2025: Blackwell (2024) → Blackwell Ultra/B300 (H2 2025) → Rubin (H2 2026) → Rubin Ultra (H2 2027) → Feynman (2028, TSMC A16, "inference-first" for agentic reasoning — very thin detail this far out). Partner availability H2 2026 is primary. Named primary partners/customers: AWS, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, OCI, CoreWeave, Dell, HPE, Lenovo, Supermicro, xAI, Cohere, Mistral AI, Perplexity. 🟠 "Full production" milestone date conflicts across secondary outlets (CES 2026 vs. GTC Taipei June 2026) — unresolved. Rubin Ultra rack naming was inconsistent in early reporting (NVL144 vs. the now-converged NVL576 "Kyber") — likely conflated with the separate, real Rubin-CPX-based NVL144 CPX product.

## Named-entity / naming corrections flagged by the research

- "72 Rubin GPUs" = 72 GPU *packages* (36 superchips × 2 GPUs), each package containing 2 compute dies — not 72 individual dies. Same package-level convention as GB200 NVL72.
- Three distinct Rubin-family SKUs must not be conflated: base **Rubin GPU** (HBM4, the NVL72 rack GPU), **Rubin CPX** (GDDR7, long-context/prefill inference, a separate product), **Rubin Ultra** (quad-die, H2 2027, NVL576 "Kyber" rack).
- Rubin Ultra's rack is **NVL576**, not NVL144 — an early/secondary mislabel that appears to conflate it with the real but distinct "Vera Rubin NVL144 CPX" product.
- The "7.5x vs GB300" performance claim belongs to the NVL144 CPX platform, not the standard Vera Rubin NVL72 — don't cite it as a general Rubin-vs-Blackwell multiplier.
