## Pre agentic SW Factories — compressed

- 🟡 **Two genuinely distinct pre-agentic senses exist, not one** — this is the headline surprise of this pass. Neither guess in the original research brief (industrial-manufacturing history vs. generic DevOps) was wrong; both turned out to be real, separately-documented usages of the exact phrase "software factory."
- 🟡 **Sense 1 — 1968–1980s industrial/manufacturing sense.** Robert Bemer proposed it at GE in 1968 as a response to the "software crisis." Hitachi was first to adopt the label (1969), then SDC (1975), then NEC/Toshiba/Fujitsu (1976–77). Michael Cusumano's 1991 book *Japan's Software Factories* is the definitive treatment. Concrete numbers: Toshiba's Fuchu Software Factory raised code reuse 13%→48% and productivity 1,390→3,100+ LOC/person-month, 1976–1985. Bemer's original paper and Cusumano's book were bibliographically confirmed but not text-read in this pass — treat exact wording as unconfirmed.
- 🟡 **A distinct, unrelated 2004 echo to flag (not use) — Greenfield & Short's *Software Factories* book** (model-driven development, visual/DSL-based app assembly). Same phrase, different meaning, no connection to the agentic throughline — mention only in passing if at all, to avoid conflating it with sense 1.
- 🟡 **Sense 2 — 2017–2022 DoD/DevSecOps sense** — the stronger candidate for a "before" picture matching the chapter's CI/CD framing, but it's a specific named government program, not generic DevOps: **Kessel Run** (Air Force's first software factory, April 2017), **Platform One** (Dec 2019, central DevSecOps toolchain), **29 DoD software factories by 2022**, and an active **Software Factory Coalition** (11 orgs) today. Working definition: automated build/test, multitenant shared pipeline, continuous authorization-to-operate — but humans still write every line of code.
- 🟠 **A dated bridge piece** ("[The End of the Assembly Line](https://www.averagegeniuses.com/the-end-of-the-assembly-line-why-the-dod-software-factory-era-is-over/)", Dec 2025) directly connects sense 2 to the agentic era: "Software Factories didn't fail; they were outpaced" — single-source but a clean, citable "old model → new model" quote.

## Agentic SW Factories — compressed

- 🟢 **Four independently-motivated sources converge on the same definition** — a solo practitioner (Addy Osmani), an agentic-coding vendor (Factory.ai), an AI-infra vendor (TrueFoundry), and a management consultancy (BCG Platinion). None of them treat "agentic" as "more automation" or "uses AI somewhere" — the line is drawn at **autonomous execution of actual SDLC work (plan/code/test/review) by agents**, vs. automation that just standardizes or speeds up a pipeline humans still run by hand. This independently strengthens (doesn't just restate) the Harness → Loop → Factory framing already in `notes/initial-notes/Software Factory.md`.
- 🟢 **Osmani's own phrasing** (directly fetched): a factory is "many harnessed loops running at once, fed by a queue of work and drained through a review gate into production, with humans owning the whole thing from above" — "not a bigger agent; it is an org chart made of loops."
- 🟢 **Factory.ai's "Factory 2.0" manifesto** (June 2026): traditional pipelines are "static, manually-triggered sequences"; an agentic factory is a "continuous feedback loop" — triage→plan→build→test→review→secure→ship→monitor, with monitoring re-feeding the loop. Three pillars: model independence, sovereign intelligence, continual learning.
- 🟡 **TrueFoundry's history bridge**: 1968 (Bemer) → 1975 (SDC) → 2004 (Greenfield/Short) → 2010s enterprise CI/CD (cites **BMW: 12,000+ developers, ~140,000 daily CI jobs** — a concrete large-scale pre-agentic CI/CD data point new to this pass) → 2026 agent-native reinterpretation.
- 🟡 **BCG Platinion**: contrasts 2022-era copilots (~30% productivity gains, humans still write/review every line) against agentic factories (agents build/test/ship around the clock). Introduces two citable human-role terms: **"harness engineering"** (designing the factory) and **"intent thinking"** (translating business needs into testable outcome descriptions). Cites vendor-sourced figures — OpenAI (~1M LOC, 3 engineers, 5 months, zero manual code) and Spotify (60–90% faster migration, 1,500+ agent-generated PRs, a claim that engineers "haven't written code since December 2025") — **flag these as single-source/vendor-reported, not independently audited**, same treatment as Factory.ai's own customer metrics in the prior research pass.

## Named-entity notes / things to get right

- Don't conflate the two pre-agentic senses — they're unrelated except for sharing the English phrase. The 1960s–80s sense is about industrializing *human* programming labor (reuse, standardized process); the 2017–2022 DoD sense is about automating a *pipeline* around human-written code. Both predate agentic coding; neither is "wrong."
- Greenfield & Short's 2004 book is a third, separate meaning (model-driven development) — worth a one-line mention at most, not a full treatment, since it doesn't connect to the chapter's before/after arc.
- Vendor/consultancy-reported metrics (Spotify, OpenAI via BCG Platinion; Factory.ai's own customer numbers from the prior pass) should be attributed explicitly as such wherever cited, not stated as independently audited fact.
